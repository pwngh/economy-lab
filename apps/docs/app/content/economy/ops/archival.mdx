The hot tables of an append-only ledger grow for the life of the economy. `economy.capacity()` reports that growth as `historySize`. Two worker sweeps govern the growth. Both are host opt-ins and do nothing until configured. The **archive** sweep moves proved history to cold storage. The **retention** sweep deletes the secondary rows whose usefulness has a horizon.

## What the archive moves

The `archive` sweep moves a strict prefix of postings in commit order — the oldest first, never a gap. It moves only history that a signed [checkpoint](/economy/concepts/integrity/) already covers, and `checkpointOlderThanMs` gates how old that coverage must be. Before the sweep copies or deletes anything, it re-derives the candidate links from their stored content and checks them against the signed heads. On a mismatch, the sweep refuses with `CHAIN.BROKEN` (or `LEDGER.UNBALANCED`) rather than move unverified history. Corrupt rows never reach the archive. The refusal is the alarm.

## The mover signs its progress

Deleting proved history would ordinarily weaken the proof, because the chain would start mid-air. The archive sweep prevents that by signing its own progress. It seals per-account archive heads under a Merkle sum-root, and it writes a signed watermark that records how far the move has reached. The pruned edge is therefore verifiable at every instant, including after a crash mid-page. The sweep resumes from the watermark, and `verifyArchiveHeads` re-checks the boundary on demand. [Offline verification](/economy/reference/offline-verification/) composes the same way. The hot chain proves back to the boundary, and the boundary's signature vouches for what moved.

## Opting in

The host provides the cold store as an `ArchiveSink`. The sink is the destination that pages are copied into before any delete. The host passes the sweep its options through the worker:

```ts
import { createWorker } from '@pwngh/economy-lab/worker';

const worker = createWorker(ports, economy, {
  archive: {
    sink: myColdStore, // an ArchiveSink: pages copied in before any delete
    checkpointOlderThanMs: 30 * 24 * 60 * 60 * 1000, // move history sealed a month ago
  },
});
```

Absent the option, the `archive` sweep reports its idle summary and touches nothing.

## The retention horizons

The `retention` sweep covers the two other lifetime-growth surfaces. Each surface sits behind its own horizon. The sweep leaves a surface untouched while its horizon is unset:

- **Idempotency rows** (`idempotencyOlderThanMs`) — a deleted key is open again. A duplicate request that arrives after deletion re-executes instead of replaying. Set the horizon beyond every window in which a caller could still retry. The [idempotency](/economy/concepts/idempotency/) guarantee holds exactly up to the horizon.
- **Settled sessions' journal rows** (`sessionsOlderThanMs`) — pruning a [session](/economy/concepts/session-netting/) removes it from the orphan sweep's enumeration and from reads. The sweep therefore verifies before it prunes. It deletes rows only for a session whose settlement evidence checks out and whose escrow remainders were refunded, and only past the horizon. Set the horizon beyond the refund and dispute window.

```ts
const worker = createWorker(ports, economy, {
  retention: {
    idempotencyOlderThanMs: 90 * 24 * 60 * 60 * 1000,
    sessionsOlderThanMs: 90 * 24 * 60 * 60 * 1000,
  },
});
```

Both sweeps ride the ordinary [worker cycle](/economy/reference/background-worker/). They run in bounded batches, they are safe to re-run, and they take one step per tick.
